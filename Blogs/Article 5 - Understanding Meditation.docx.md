**Meditation: Learning to Be with Yourself**

For a very long time, I thought meditation was about making the mind quiet.

I believed that if I was doing it correctly, my thoughts would slow down, my mind would become peaceful, and I would somehow reach a state where nothing bothered me.

I was wrong.

When I first started meditating, my mind was anything but quiet. There were thoughts, memories, emotions, restlessness, distractions and days when I simply did not feel like sitting down at all.

And honestly, I went on and off.

There were days when I was consistent and days when I completely abandoned the practice. Sometimes I thought I wasn't doing it right. Sometimes I expected meditation to change something immediately.

Over time, I understood that meditation was never really about **stopping my thoughts**.

It was about learning to **observe them without becoming them**.

That distinction changed everything for me.

Meditation gave me a little distance from my own mind. I began noticing how quickly a thought could become an emotion, how an emotion could influence a reaction, and how often I was responding to life from patterns I hadn't even consciously recognised.

With regular practice, I began becoming more aware of myself—not just when I was sitting with my eyes closed, but in everyday life.

I became more aware of my reactions.

More aware of what disturbed my peace.

More aware of the stories I was telling myself.

More aware of what I needed and what I was simply accustomed to tolerating.

This is where I believe the deeper value of meditation lies.

It can create a space between **what happens and how we respond to it**.

And that space can be incredibly powerful.

Meditation can support us in managing stress, becoming more focused, regulating emotions and developing greater self-awareness. It can also become a beautiful way of reconnecting with the body and noticing what is happening within us.

But I don't think meditation should become another thing we feel pressured to “do perfectly.”

Some days, sitting for twenty minutes may feel effortless.

Some days, five minutes may feel like enough.

Some days, the mind may feel peaceful.

Other days, it may feel chaotic.

None of that means you have failed at meditation.

The practice is simply to return.

Return to the breath.

Return to the body.

Return to the present moment.

And, perhaps most importantly, return to yourself.

**How to Begin**

You don't need a special room, expensive equipment or hours of free time to begin.

Find a comfortable and reasonably quiet place. Sit in a way that feels natural to your body. Close your eyes if that feels comfortable and bring your attention to your breath.

You will think.

Let yourself think.

You will get distracted.

Notice it.

You will remember something you had completely forgotten.

Notice that too.

And then gently come back.

There is no need to fight your mind.

There is no need to judge yourself for having thoughts.

**The noticing is the practice.**

Start with a few minutes. Do it consistently rather than trying to do it perfectly. Over time, meditation can become less about escaping from life and more about becoming present enough to actually experience it.

For me, meditation became much more than a wellness practice. It became a way of understanding myself. And I think that is where healing begins. Not when the mind becomes completely silent.

But when we become quite enough to finally hear what it has been trying to tell us.
